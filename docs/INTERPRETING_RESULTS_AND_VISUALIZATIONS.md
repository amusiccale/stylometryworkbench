# Purpose

This guide explains how to interpret similarity metrics, attribution rankings, radar charts, and feature-difference visualizations generated by the Stylometric Fingerprint Workbench.

Stylometric analysis is based on the principle that writers often exhibit recurring and measurable linguistic habits. Authorship attribution therefore relies on patterns of evidence rather than absolute proof of identity. Modern stylometric research generally treats attribution as probabilistic and comparative rather than definitive. 【1-165d19】【2-ec792d】

---

# Fundamental Principle

Stylometric evidence should be interpreted as evidence rather than proof.

```text
High similarity
≠
same author

Low similarity
≠
different author
```

The goal is to accumulate converging evidence from multiple independent signals.

# Part I
# Statistical Measures

---

## Similarity Score

### What It Measures

The similarity score estimates how closely two stylistic profiles resemble one another.

Higher values indicate greater stylistic resemblance.

---

### How To Interpret

Interpret similarity comparatively rather than absolutely.

Useful questions include:

```text
Which author or document has the highest score?

How large is the gap between first and second place?

Do several candidates cluster together?
```

The relative ranking of candidates is often more informative than the absolute similarity value itself. 【1-165d19】【2-ec792d】

---

### Important Caveat

```text
High similarity
≠
same author
```

Similarity measures stylistic resemblance rather than confirmed authorship.

## Distance Score

### What It Measures

Distance quantifies stylistic separation.

```text
Lower distance
=
more similar

Higher distance
=
more different
```

---

### How To Interpret

Example:

```text
Author A
Distance = 0.21

Author B
Distance = 0.47

Author C
Distance = 0.88
```

Author A is the closest stylistic match.

Distance measures are among the most common approaches in authorship attribution research. 【2-ec792d】【1-165d19】

## Attribution Rankings

### What They Measure

Attribution rankings order candidate authors according to stylistic similarity.

Example:

```text
1. Author 7
2. Author 12
3. Author 9
```

---

### How To Interpret

Ask:

```text
How far ahead is Rank 1?

Is Rank 2 nearly tied?

Does Rank 1 remain stable when
different feature groups are selected?
```

---

### Workbench Interpretation Guideline

```text
Large gaps between Rank 1 and Rank 2
may indicate stronger attribution signals.

Small gaps may indicate uncertainty.
```

This is a practical Workbench guideline rather than a universal stylometric rule.

# Part II
# Visualizations

---

## Radar Charts

### Purpose

Radar charts provide a visual summary of stylistic profiles across multiple feature dimensions.

Each axis represents a stylometric feature.

---

### What To Look For

#### Similar Shapes

```text
Generally similar stylistic profiles.
```

When multiple features align visually, the profiles may share stylistic characteristics.

---

#### Divergent Spikes

```text
Potential stylistic differences.
```

Large spikes often indicate dimensions in which one profile differs substantially from another.

---

#### Outliers

```text
Features that stand out strongly.
```

Outliers may represent distinctive characteristics of a writer, text, or corpus.

---

### Display Modes

#### Raw

Displays original measurements.

Best for:

```text
Numerical inspection.
```

---

#### Log

Compresses large values.

Best for:

```text
Reducing scale differences.
```

---

#### Relative

Scales values relative to the largest displayed value.

Best for:

```text
Comparing proportions visually.
```

---

#### Log Relative

Applies logarithmic compression followed by relative scaling.

Recommended for:

```text
Most exploratory analysis.
```

---

### Important Caveat

Radar charts emphasize visual shape.

```text
Visual similarity does not necessarily
equal statistical similarity.
```

Radar charts should never be used as the sole basis for attribution conclusions.

## Difference Charts

### Purpose

Difference charts explain WHY two profiles differ.

Rather than asking:

```text
How similar are these profiles?
```

difference charts ask:

```text
Which features contribute most strongly
to the observed differences?
```

---

### How To Read Them

#### Positive Values

```text
Feature is higher in Profile A.
```

---

#### Negative Values

```text
Feature is higher in Profile B.
```

---

#### Large Bars

Large bars represent the strongest stylistic differences.

These features often merit closer inspection.

---

### Interpretive Value

Difference charts can be more informative than a single similarity score because they identify the specific dimensions that drive similarity or separation.

Research increasingly emphasizes interpretability and feature-level explanation in stylometric analysis. 【3-e695ec】【4-0e2369】

---

### Display Modes

#### Raw

Displays original differences.

---

#### Log

Compresses large differences.

---

#### Relative

Scales differences relative to the largest observed difference.

---

#### Log Relative

Recommended.

Combines:

```text
Log compression

plus

Relative scaling
```

to reveal both large and moderate stylistic differences.

---

# Part IV
# Confidence and Uncertainty

---

## Strong Evidence Occurs When Multiple Indicators Agree

The strongest stylometric interpretations typically arise when several independent indicators point toward the same conclusion.

Example:

```text
High Similarity

Low Distance

Strong Attribution Ranking

Comparable Radar Shapes

Small Difference Profile
```

Together these provide stronger evidence than any individual signal in isolation.

---

## Conflicting Indicators Merit Investigation

Example:

```text
High Similarity

Large Difference Bars

Weak Ranking Separation
```

Such situations should be explored rather than ignored.

Possible causes include:

```text
Genre effects

Corpus imbalance

Topic influence

Mixed stylistic signals

Feature selection effects
```

# Final Reminder

Stylometric analysis provides evidence rather than certainty.

The strongest conclusions typically emerge when:

```text
Multiple measurements

Multiple visualizations

Multiple feature groups
```

all support the same interpretation.

A stylometric result should therefore be treated as part of an argument rather than as definitive proof of authorship. 【1-165d19】【2-ec792d】【3-e695ec】【4-0e2369】
